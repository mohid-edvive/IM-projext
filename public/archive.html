00 — Overview

What is Investigo?

Investigo is a web-based gamified financial literacy platform. The name pairs invest with the Latin investigo — to track, to trace, to pursue evidence with deliberate attention. It combines a 60-day curriculum, a historically accurate market simulator, a knowledge-gated earn-to-invest mechanic, and a personalized analytics dashboard.

The central argument: the crisis of hype-driven investing is a design problem. The solution is a platform where the architecture itself enforces the lesson — you cannot invest until you have demonstrated you understand what you are investing in.

01 — Process

Seven Months of Making

The project went through five completely different directions before arriving at the final platform. Each pivot was driven by real feedback, real user data, or an honest moment of realizing I was solving the wrong problem.

October 2025 — Phase 1
Finding the Problem
The earliest direction was broad: playful finance, gamified, accessible to beginners. Advisors pushed me to research before building — identify the audience, conduct interviews, write a UX paper. I was thinking about financial literacy in emerging markets and about making investing feel less like a spreadsheet and more like something human. Goals: 5 user interviews, competitive analysis, define the audience.
Research Competitive Analysis Audience Definition
Early November 2025 — Phase 2
Prototype 1 — Stock Seer
First working prototype: a DCF (Discounted Cash Flow) valuation tool. Type in a company name, get a verdict — undervalued, overvalued, or fairly priced. Built on Lovable with static data. The idea came from my VC background: valuation logic is always hidden in spreadsheets. I wanted to make it visible and teachable. What I learned: simplicity builds trust. But it had a ceiling — it was a calculator, not a learning platform.

Mid November 2025 — Phase 3
Prototype 2 — Early Alpha Finder
After in-class feedback I ran a "5 alternative meanings" exercise and pivoted. Stock Seer became an Early Alpha Finder — a tool to spot undervalued companies before Wall Street notices. Added an Alpha Score, Hidden Compounders watchlist, Catalyst Timeline, and Scenario Modeling. The framing shifted from "understand valuations" to "build wealth." It felt more exciting. It also felt like I was solving the wrong problem.
hidden-compounders.lovable.app
Prototype 2 Alpha Score Watchlist Scenario Modeling
Late November 2025 — Phase 4
Prototype 3 — Calm Money Jars
I ran the "Why?" process again and hit something uncomfortable: even if you pick the right stocks, bad money management destroys you. Alpha without allocation is fragile. People panic-sell, over-invest, forget about liquidity. So I pivoted completely — away from stock picking into money management. Three jars: Now (bills, emergencies), Soon (mid-term goals), Later (long-term compounding). Gentle nudges, drawdown guardrails, savings funnels. The most counterintuitive prototype — and the most useful design lesson.
calm-money-jars.lovable.app
Prototype 3 Money Management Behavioral Design
Early December 2025 — Phase 5
Prototype 4 — Flip & Fun
The assignment: break your own idea. I ran two exercises. Reversal: flipped Calm Money Jars into "Stress Seer" — scary alerts, doomsday what-if scenarios. Random stimulation: random word was "Garden." Built a Money Garden where dollars are seeds, Now money grows like herbs, Later money grows like oak trees. The lesson: finance apps don't have to look like spreadsheets. They can be emotional, visual, even strange — that's what makes them stick. This thinking directly influenced Investigo's final design.

User Research — Three Interviews

Kashpia (25, product manager): wants micro-learning, no jargon, human and habit-forming. Atiq (22, engineering student, invests $100/month): wants chat, not courses — personalized to his exact situation. Faisal (CS student): wants a sandbox, not a curriculum — learn by changing variables and seeing results. Three people. Three completely different mental models. The Big Pivot — Duolingo for Stock Trading
Threw out everything. Built a "Duolingo for Stock Trading" on Replit over a weekend: 25 interactive lessons, leaderboard, profiles, quizzes, games. This was the moment the current project direction was born. Learning stock trading should feel as smooth and habit-forming as learning a language. New ideas surfaced fast — multiplayer trading battles, daily market missions, simulated investment challenges. The first prototype that felt like it could actually become something.

AI Agent Experiment
The interviews pushed me toward something more conversational. Built a Chatbase AI agent trained on NVIDIA investor relations data — users learn through chat rather than structured lessons. This experiment didn't become the final product, but it gave me two things: clarity on where AI fits in financial education, and confirmation that the structured lesson approach was actually stronger than pure conversational AI for beginners.

02 — Research

Precedents & Academic Grounding

Four projects shaped the intellectual architecture of Investigo — each one teaching a specific lesson about what to build and what to avoid.

SIFMA Foundation · 1977
The Stock Market Game
The longest-running financial simulation in American education, 4M+ students annually. Research confirms significantly greater financial knowledge gains over matched control groups (Mandell, 2009).
"Real stakes inside a safe container. I kept the principle, added the prerequisite curriculum that was missing."
Luis von Ahn · 2011
Duolingo
500M+ users sustained by design, not willpower. Von Ahn's core insight: motivation — not content quality — is the binding constraint in self-directed education (von Ahn, 2013).
"The earn-to-invest mechanic is Duolingo's XP system — except the reward is investable capital, not a badge."
UW Center for Game Science · 2008
Foldit
Players solved a 15-year-old HIV protein structure problem in 10 days. The team chose scientific accuracy over simplified gameplay — and it worked (Cooper et al., 2010).
"I simulated real historical price data, not randomized markets. The fidelity is the lesson."
Investopedia · 2000s
Stock Simulator
Equity simulations improve knowledge scores by 42% on average (Moffit et al., 2010). But the simulator and education exist as entirely separate products — users can trade without reading anything.
"I inverted this completely: curriculum completion is the only path to the simulator."

User Interviews

Three formal interviews conducted November–December 2025. Each reshaped the project in a different direction.

K
Kashpia, 25
Product Manager · AI Startup · San Diego
Confirmed the Duolingo-for-investing direction but demanded nuance. Bounces off investing content when jargon hits too fast. Wants micro-learning — 5–10 minutes, small wins, one concept at a time, human tone, real-life context. Shaped the entire pacing and voice of the final curriculum.
A
Atiq, 22
Engineering Student · Invests $100/month
Didn't want levels or courses — wanted chat. Learns by asking questions in his own words and getting answers that match his level and his specific situation. Said explanations should reference that he only invests $100/month. Introduced the personalization angle that became the analytics dashboard.
F
Faisal
CS Student
Didn't want chat or lessons — wanted a sandbox. Change variables, see results, test scenarios, learn through cause and effect. Thinks in systems. His feedback pushed the simulator toward higher fidelity and more interactive scenario framing at each historical event.

04 — User Testing

12 Remote Sessions

All sessions were remote — the course was fully online. Zoom and Google Meet, think-aloud protocol, structured debrief questions. Three rounds across the semester: early, mid-stage, and near-final.

✓
The simulator's emotional power confirmed
Multiple participants — including self-described experienced investors — sold during the COVID crash sequence, locked in losses, then watched the market recover past their entry point in the same session. The experiential lesson landed in a way no reading could replicate.
"Oh. That is what people mean when they say do not sell the bottom." — Participant, 24, 2 years on Robinhood
⚠
Curriculum completion friction — 6 of 12 testers
Users with prior investing knowledge were frustrated by foundational lessons before simulator access. The mechanic is architecturally correct but creates real friction for users who arrive with prior knowledge. Resolution in development: a placement assessment at onboarding.
"I know what a stock is. I do not need a lesson on what a stock is before I can practice buying one." — Participant, Robinhood user, 2 years experience
◈
Investor identity resonates more than instruction
Participants across all skill levels paused to study their analytics results. Several asked to screenshot their investor type classification. Being told what kind of investor you are — based on your own decisions — resonated far more than any explicit lesson content.
↗
Mobile responsiveness broken — top complaint from walkthrough
Almost everyone tried the demo on their phone. Elements overlapped, buttons pushed off-screen, scrolling unpredictable, app freezing. The concept is meant to be mobile-accessible. This became the immediate top priority for fixes after the walkthrough.
+
XP gates and Daily Wisdom AI got strong positive response
The lesson → XP → unlocked assets loop felt motivating and intuitive to most testers. The Gemini-powered Daily Wisdom AI mentor was noted for how calm and non-trading-app-like it felt — a deliberate design choice validated by real users.

05 — Reflection

What Building This Taught Me

Seven months of building Investigo taught me things I could not have learned by reading about them — which is appropriately on-theme for a project about experiential learning. The most important discovery was how emotionally powerful the simulator became. I designed it as an educational tool. In practice it functioned as something closer to a mirror.

The most humbling: six of twelve testers hit friction I designed into the system deliberately. The earn-to-invest mechanic is architecturally correct — and it annoyed half my test group. Good design and correct design are not always the same thing. The placement assessment I am now building is not a workaround. It is the design becoming more honest about who actually shows up.

The most useful lesson from the early prototypes: the Calm Money Jars pivot in Prototype 3 forced me to think about what investing education is actually for. Not picking winners. Surviving long enough to benefit when you do. That reframing lives inside Investigo's curriculum today even though the jars are gone.

The hardest thing to admit: Investigo can make you a more informed investor. It cannot change the environment in which you invest. Social media platforms and brokerage apps are designed to reward emotional reactivity over deliberate analysis. That is a larger design problem this platform alone cannot solve. Worth naming — not as a reason to stop, but as an honest acknowledgment of where the work ends and a harder problem begins.

06 — Future Steps

Where This Goes Next

Six priorities — all direct responses to specific findings from testing and production, not a wishlist.

01
Placement Assessment
20-question onboarding diagnostic. Bypass lessons you already know, still receive the equivalent virtual currency. Directly addresses friction raised by 6 of 12 testers.
02
Mobile Responsiveness
Fix what broke. The platform is meant to be accessible on any device. Elements overlapping, buttons off-screen, freezing on phones. Top priority before any public launch.
03
Real-Time Simulation
After the historical simulation, apply learning to live market conditions. One-week delayed analytics preserves the reflective structure that historical hindsight currently provides.
04
Empirical Outcome Study
Pre/post assessment measuring financial literacy, hype susceptibility, and investment confidence. The central claim needs data, not just argument.
05
Behavioral Benchmarking
Cohort comparisons on process metrics — timing discipline, risk management, consistency — not raw returns. Reward decision quality over luck.
06
Fintech Integration
Payment infrastructure, subscription tiers, wallet top-ups. AppSumo launch strategy for early adopters. Turn the capstone prototype into a real product.
